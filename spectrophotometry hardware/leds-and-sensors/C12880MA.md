# Hamamatsu C12880MA mini-spectrometer

The UV-Vis spectrometer head behind every `C12-*` component on the Jimini. Fingertip-sized grating spectrometer with a 288-pixel CMOS linear image sensor. Its NIR sibling `C14384MA` sits behind the `C14-*` components — see [[device]] for the component naming.

Datasheet: [`datasheets/C12880MA-C16767MA-datasheet.pdf`](datasheets/C12880MA-C16767MA-datasheet.pdf) (KACC1226E, covers C12880MA / C16767MA / C16767MA-01).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **`Vsat` is relative to `Vo`, not absolute.** Datasheet footnote 8: the saturation voltage *at the video output terminal* is `Vo + Vsat`. Their worked example — `Vo = 0.5 V`, `Vsat = 4.3 V` — gives **4.8 V**. Using 4.3 V as the ceiling under-reads it by half a volt.

> [!WARNING]
> **The ceiling is part-specific.** `Vsat` spans 3.6–4.6 V and `Vo` spans 0.3–0.9 V, so the absolute saturation point ranges from ~3.9 V to ~5.5 V unit to unit — the latter above the 5 V rail. There is no single count value that means "saturated" across a fleet; it has to be characterised per device or bounded conservatively.

## What this means in ADC counts

The sensor is analog: it outputs a video voltage which the host digitises. **The count ceiling is a property of the Jimini's ADC and analog front end, not of the C12880MA.** Assuming a 16-bit ADC with a 5 V reference:

| Quantity | Voltage | ≈ counts |
|---|---|---|
| Output offset pedestal `Vo` (typ) | 0.5 V | ~6 550 |
| Saturation `Vo + Vsat` (typ) | 4.8 V | **~62 900** |
| ADC full scale | 5.0 V | 65 535 |

Observed on the Jimini fleet: highest raw count ever seen is **65 114**, and the offset pedestal is consistent with `C12-VISD-100.0mA` sitting at ~13 100 counts (≈1.0 V, barely 0.5 V above the pedestal).

> [!CAUTION]
> Because `Vo + Vsat` varies per unit, saturating pixels do **not** pile up on one count value. Across 1010 measured components the most-repeated maximum occurred 4 times, with 128 distinct maxima above 64 000. **Absence of a pile-up is not evidence of no saturation** on this part — that inference is only valid for a hard digital clip.

## Measured saturation on the Jimini fleet (2026-08)

Several `C12` components are driven to a level chosen for water and reach the ceiling. Established by a paired drive-current test — the same emitter is acquired at 50 mA and 100 mA in the same scan, so doubling the current must roughly double an unsaturated peak:

| emitter | median raw peak @50 mA | @100 mA | ratio | flat pixels @50 → @100 (of ~15 in window) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **365** | 63 892 | 63 910 | **1.000** | 3 → **6** |
| **405** | 63 295 | 63 853 | **1.002** | 2 → 4 |
| 455 | 57 654 | 63 676 | 1.096 | 1 → 4 |

101 scans, 9 devices, p10–p90 of the 365 ratio is 0.999–1.002. Doubling the drive current moves 365 by **0.0%**: hard saturation. The plateau *widens* with current while the peak value does not move — extra light spreads the clipped region sideways because the top cannot rise.

### Per-device ceiling and the working threshold

Where the ratio is ~1 the band is clipped, so that device's peak **is** its ceiling. Measured that way:

| | counts |
|---|---|
| Lowest device ceiling | 62 819 |
| Highest device ceiling | 64 907 |
| Spread | 2 088 (3.3%) |
| Datasheet typ `Vo + Vsat` at 16-bit/5 V | 62 914 |

The `datascience` repo uses **`SATURATION_LEVEL = 62 000`** — about 1% below the lowest device seen, and just under the datasheet typical. Erring low is deliberate: a threshold above a device's own ceiling would call its clipped scans healthy.

| band | saturated | band | saturated |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R405@100` | 92.4% | `455@50` | 23.1% |

| `455@100` | 79.0% | `visd@100Blue` / `visd@100White` | 0.0% |

`455@50`, both `C12-VIS-1.7mA` bands, `vis@2.5Blue` and both `C12-VISD-100.0mA` bands retain real headroom.

> [!CAUTION]
> Seven of thirteen measured bands are clipped on three-quarters or more of scans, so their peak height reports the ADC ceiling rather than the emitter. The fix is upstream — a shorter integration time or lower drive current for those components — not in processing.

> [!NOTE]
> Detect saturation on **raw counts, before dark subtraction and resampling**. Per-pixel dark subtraction gives each clipped pixel a slightly different value and destroys the plateau; resampling to a finer grid smears what is left. A flatness test run on processed spectra reports ~0% saturation on data that is demonstrably saturated. Use a tolerance around 0.5% of the peak, not 0.1% — analog compression rounds the top rather than hard-clipping it, and 0.1% of 63 800 counts is only ~2.7× the readout noise.

## Spectral resolution versus pixel pitch

288 pixels over 340–850 nm is **~1.77 nm per pixel**, while the optical resolution is **12 nm FWHM typ**. Each pixel therefore reports a weighted integral over a ~12 nm-wide instrument line shape (ILS) centred on its own wavelength, and the spectrum is **oversampled roughly 7×** relative to the information it actually carries. Adjacent pixels are strongly correlated; resampling onto a 1 nm grid adds interpolation points, not resolution.

Consequences worth knowing:

- A measured emission width is the true width **convolved** with the ILS. For near-Gaussian shapes, `fwhmMeasured² ≈ fwhmTrue² + fwhmILS²`. At a 12 nm ILS, a measured 19 nm LED is really ~14.7 nm wide, and measured width compresses true variation by ~25%.
- **Peak position is unaffected** by a symmetric ILS — convolution preserves the centroid — so a peak-wavelength measurement needs no correction.
- The ILS is a per-unit property (slit, grating, alignment), so part of any device-to-device spread in measured width is spectrometer, not emitter.
- Deconvolution (Richardson–Lucy, Van Cittert, regularised Wiener) can sharpen this, but needs a measured ILS — a narrow line source such as an Hg/Ar pen lamp — and amplifies noise. A quadrature subtraction of the ILS width is the cheap 80% version and needs only one number per unit.

> [!TIP]
> Hamamatsu supplies **per-unit wavelength conversion factors** on the final inspection sheet (pixel index → nm polynomial). Datasheet footnote 1 states explicitly that **no factor for converting A/D counts to light level is provided** — the part is not radiometrically calibrated, which is why device-to-device comparison must be relative.

## Gaps

- **ADC width and reference voltage are unconfirmed.** The count↔voltage mapping above assumes 16-bit at 5 V. Everything in the counts table scales with that assumption.
- **No per-unit `Vo`/`Vsat` characterisation.** The ceiling is known to vary 3.9–5.5 V by spec but has not been measured per device; a conservative global threshold is currently the only option.
- **ILS never measured.** No narrow-line capture exists for any unit, so the 12 nm typ figure is the datasheet's rather than ours, and no per-unit width correction is possible yet.
- **Integration time is fixed at 116 µs fleet-wide**, so the database offers no exposure-linearity leverage; the drive-current pairs were used instead.
